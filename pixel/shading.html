Shading, dithering & anti-aliasing

Pick a light source

Choose one light direction, usually top-left, and keep it the same for every sprite in the game. Shade the form as if it were a 3D object: surfaces facing the light are lighter, and surfaces facing away are darker.

Dithering

Dithering mixes two colours in a pattern (often a checkerboard) to suggest a colour in between them, or a texture.

  • ✓ Good for: large gradients (skies), textures (stone, fabric), and retro palettes with very few colours.
  • ✗ Avoid it on small sprites and moving objects, where it flickers and turns into noise.

Manual anti-aliasing (AA)

AA softens a stair-step edge by placing intermediate colours in the corners of the steps. Use it on internal lines and curves. Leave the outer silhouette crisp, especially for sprites that move over different backgrounds, because AA pixels blended toward one background colour look wrong over another.

EXERCISE

Draw a 16×16 rock with a 4-colour ramp lit from the top-left. Add 2–3 AA pixels where the internal crack meets the top surface. Compare it with a version without AA when shown at 1× and 4×.
