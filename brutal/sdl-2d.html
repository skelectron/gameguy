Pixel-perfect 2D with SDL

You will learn
  • How to open an SDL window with a hardware-accelerated 2D renderer
  • How to render at a tiny "virtual" resolution (e.g. 320×180) and scale it up crisply
  • How to load PNG sprites with Sdl.Image and draw them with nearest-neighbour filtering
  • How to handle input with the SDL event queue and keyboard state

GLFW plus OpenGL gives you complete control, but you have to write shaders just to draw a square. SDL3, wrapped as Brutal.Sdl, includes a 2D renderer that draws textures and rectangles on the GPU for you. It's ideal for pixel-art games, and it's what we'll use for the capstone.

Window, renderer and vsync

Why IntegerScale?

Pixel art only looks right when every art pixel becomes an exact N×N block of screen pixels. If you scale 320×180 to a 1366-pixel-wide window (×4.27), some art pixels become 4 screen pixels wide and some become 5. That causes "pixel wobble" and uneven lines. SdlRendererLogicalPresentation.IntegerScale picks the largest whole-number scale that fits and adds black bars around the rest. You can also use Letterbox (keeps the aspect ratio but allows fractional scaling) or Stretch.

CHOOSING A VIRTUAL RESOLUTION

320×180 and 640×360 divide evenly into 720p, 1080p, 1440p and 4K (16:9). 256×224 is the classic console size. See Canvas size & palettes.

Sprites with Sdl.Image

  • SdlScaleMode.Nearest is essential. Linear filtering blurs your art. SDL3 also has a Pixelart mode that keeps edges sharp but smooth at non-integer scales.
  • Round positions before drawing. Sub-pixel positions on pixel art cause shimmering.
  • The src rectangle selects one cell from a sprite sheet. Animation is just changing which cell you pick over time (see Sprite animation).

Keyboard: events vs state

Scancodes are physical key positions, so SdlScancode.A is the key in the "A" position on any keyboard layout. That's what you want for WASD on AZERTY keyboards.

Ownership order

In samples/Demo/DemoSdl.cs, the objects are declared "in dependency order, so the using declarations dispose them in reverse": window → renderer → textures. A texture belongs to a renderer, and a renderer belongs to a window, so they must be destroyed in the reverse order.
