2D physics with Box2D

You will learn
  • Box2D concepts: world, bodies, shapes and steps
  • Static vs dynamic bodies
  • Units: why you should simulate in metres, not pixels
  • How to read body transforms to draw rotated boxes

Box2D is a famous 2D physics engine that has been used in thousands of games. Brutal.Box2D binds Box2D v3. For 3D, use Brutal.Jolt instead (see HelloJolt and DemoJolt).

Headless first: drop a box

Key ideas

ConceptMeaning
WorldHolds every body and owns gravity. Step(dt, subSteps) advances the simulation.
BodyHas a position, rotation and velocity. Static (default) never moves, Dynamic is moved by forces, and Kinematic is moved by you.
ShapeCollision geometry attached to a body. MakeBox takes half width and height.
SleepBodies at rest go to sleep, so they cost nothing until something touches them.
METRES, NOT PIXELS

Box2D is tuned for objects between about 0.1 and 10 metres. A 32-pixel crate should be a 1-metre box, not a 32-metre one. Keep physics in metres and convert when you draw: screen = world * PIXELS_PER_METRE.

When not to use a physics engine

Precise platformers (think tight jumps and pixel-perfect ledges) often feel better with hand-written collision: AABBs plus tile checks. Realistic physics gives you momentum and bounce you didn't ask for. Use Box2D for physics-driven fun such as stacking, ragdolls, vehicles and destruction. The capstone uses simple AABBs, and the engine track explains why in Integrating physics & audio.
