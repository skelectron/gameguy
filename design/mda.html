The MDA framework

MDA (Mechanics, Dynamics, Aesthetics; Hunicke, LeBlanc & Zubek, 2004) is a simple model for thinking about how rules turn into feelings.

  • Mechanics: the rules and data. JUMP_SPEED = 330, "coins give 10 points", "enemies respawn after 30 s".
  • Dynamics: what happens when players interact with those rules over time. Speedrunners finding routes, players camping respawn points, economies inflating.
  • Aesthetics: the emotional responses. Challenge, discovery, fellowship, expression, fantasy, narrative, sensation, or submission (relaxing play).

Why it matters

You can only edit mechanics, but players only care about aesthetics. Designing well means reasoning through dynamics: "If I add a timer (mechanic), players will rush and take risks (dynamic), which creates tension and excitement (aesthetic)."

EXERCISE

Pick a target aesthetic for your game, e.g. "tense discovery". Propose three mechanics that should produce it, and predict the dynamics each one creates. Then predict one unwanted dynamic (an exploit) and a mechanic that would counter it.
