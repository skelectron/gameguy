Level design fundamentals

Levels teach

The best tutorials don't feel like tutorials. A level teaches a mechanic by letting the player discover it safely, then tests it, and then combines it with other mechanics.

INTRODUCE → DEVELOP → TWIST → CONCLUDE
  1. Introduce: show the mechanic where failing costs nothing (a small gap over solid ground).
  2. Develop: raise the stakes (a real pit).
  3. Twist: combine it with something new (a gap with a moving platform).
  4. Conclude: a final test that proves mastery, followed by a reward.

Guiding the player

  • Lines of sight: let players see their goal before they can reach it.
  • Breadcrumbs: coins and collectibles trace the intended path, including where to jump.
  • Contrast: walkable surfaces should read clearly against the background (see readability).
  • Landmarks: memorable shapes help players find their way in larger levels.
  • Gating: show a locked door early and give the key later. That creates anticipation.

Pacing

Alternate tension and release. Put a calm area after a hard section, and a breather before a boss. Plot intensity against time and aim for rising waves, not a flat line or a constant spike.

Grey-boxing

Build levels from plain blocks first, as in Coin Dash's string map. Test the gameplay before spending any time on art. In BRUTAL, a text-file level format plus hot reload (assets lesson) gives you a very fast loop: edit the text, save, and play the new level immediately.

EXERCISE

Design three Coin Dash levels that teach "short hop vs full jump" (variable jump height). Level 1 introduces it, level 2 develops it with low ceilings, and level 3 twists it by combining it with pits. Grey-box them as text, playtest them, and revise.
