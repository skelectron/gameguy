Difficulty & balancing

Flow

Players are most engaged in flow: when the challenge roughly matches their skill. If the challenge is too high they get anxious, and if it's too low they get bored. Because skill grows as people play, difficulty should rise too, in waves rather than a straight line.

Levers you can pull

LeverExamples
PrecisionPlatform width, gap length, hitbox size
Time pressureTimers, enemy speed, reaction windows
ComplexityNumber of things to track at once
PunishmentCheckpoint spacing, lives, what you lose on death
ResourcesHealth, ammo, money

Lowering the punishment (more checkpoints) often lets you keep the challenge high without frustrating players.

Numbers in spreadsheets, not code

Put balance data in data files (JSON, CSV), not in constants. Designers can tweak them without recompiling, and with hot reload they can do it while the game is running.

Useful formulas

  • Time-to-kill = enemy HP ÷ player DPS. Keep it in a target range for each enemy tier.
  • Exponential growth (cost = base × 1.15^level) for idle and RPG progression. Pair it with growing rewards.
  • Dominant strategies: if one option is always best, the other options don't matter. Give each option a cost or a counter.
EXERCISE

Build a spreadsheet for a 10-wave arena: enemy count, HP and speed per wave, plus player DPS per upgrade level. Plot time-to-clear for each wave and adjust the numbers until it rises in waves, with an easy wave after every boss wave.
