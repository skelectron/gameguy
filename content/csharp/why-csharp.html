Why C# for games?

C# runs Unity games, Godot's .NET mode, Stardew Valley (on MonoGame/FNA), Celeste, Terraria and now BRUTAL titles such as Kitten Space Agency and RimWorld's Waylith engine. It sits in a sweet spot:

  • Productive like a scripting language: garbage collection, great tools, fast compile times and a huge standard library.
  • Fast like a systems language when you need it: structs, Span<T>, stackalloc, pointers, SIMD and direct native interop.
  • Cross-platform: .NET 10 runs on Windows, Linux and macOS, with Native AOT when you want it.

Two kinds of C#

Most tutorials teach "business C#", which is full of classes, LINQ, async and allocations everywhere. That's fine for tools and menus. Engine and gameplay hot paths use a different style:

Business-style C#Game/engine-style C#
class everythingstruct for small data (vectors, particles, components)
List<T> + LINQArrays, Span<T> and for loops; LINQ outside hot paths
string concatenationUTF-8 spans and pooled builders (BRUTAL's String8)
Let the GC clean upAvoid per-frame allocations, and dispose native resources deterministically
Exceptions for control flowReturn values; exceptions only for truly exceptional failures

This track teaches both, with a focus on the second style, because that's what you need to read and write BRUTAL code.
