Game feel & juice

Game feel is how controlling the game feels physically: responsive or sluggish, crunchy or floaty. Juice is the extra feedback that makes actions satisfying. Neither changes the rules, but both change how the game feels to play.

Try it

Toggle each effect and jump around (←→ to move, Space to jump, after clicking the canvas). Turn them all off, then all on.

The feel toolkit

Responsiveness

  • Acceleration and deceleration: quick to start, fast to stop. Raw instant velocity feels robotic, and very slow acceleration feels like driving on ice.
  • Input forgiveness: coyote time, jump buffering, and slightly generous hitboxes for the player (and slightly smaller ones for enemy bullets).
  • Variable jump height: hold to jump higher. This gives the player fine control.
  • Snappier falls: higher gravity on the way down feels less floaty.

Feedback (juice)

  • Squash & stretch on jump and land.
  • Particles: dust, sparks, coin sparkles.
  • Screen shake, small and short. Always offer an option to turn it off.
  • Hit-stop: freeze for 2–6 frames on big impacts.
  • Sound on every action, with a small random pitch variation (±5%).
  • Easing on UI and camera motion instead of linear movement.
DON'T OVERDO IT

Juice should support the gameplay, not hide it. Too much shake causes nausea and makes the screen hard to read. Expose the key tuning values in a debug UI (ImGui) and tune them by playing, not by reasoning about them.

EXERCISE

Add squash and stretch, landing dust and a coin sparkle to Coin Dash. Ask a friend to play the version without juice and then the version with it, and write down what they say.
