The main loop & fixed timestep

You will learn
  • Three ways to advance time, and why engines prefer fixed steps
  • The accumulator, the spiral of death, and how to clamp it
  • How to render between steps with interpolation

Three strategies

StrategyUpdate callProsCons
VariableUpdate(realDt)Simple; always smoothPhysics differs per frame rate; lag spikes cause tunnelling; not reproducible
Fixed (locked)Update(1/60) once per frameDeterministicThe game runs in slow motion on slow machines and too fast at 144 Hz
Fixed + accumulatorUpdate(1/60) 0..N times per frameDeterministic and runs at real speedNeeds interpolation to look smooth on high-refresh screens

Live demo

Each ball is thrown with the same starting velocity. Lower the render FPS or add lag spikes and watch the variable-dt ball. Its arc and peak height change, and it can fall through the floor. The fixed-step ball always follows the same path.

The spiral of death

If one simulation step takes longer than STEP, every frame needs more steps than the last. The frame gets slower, it needs even more steps, and the game locks up. Clamping frame to 0.25 s means the game slows down instead of freezing. If this happens regularly, your simulation is too expensive for your step rate.

Interpolation

At 144 Hz with 60 Hz updates, some frames have no update at all, so without interpolation objects stutter. Rendering Lerp(prev, curr, alpha) shows a smooth position that runs up to one step behind the simulation, which is too little to feel. Toggle "Interpolate fixed" in the demo at a high FPS with a low step rate to see the difference. For pixel-art games, round the interpolated position to whole pixels at draw time.

Where things go

  • Inside the fixed step: physics, gameplay rules, AI, anything that should be deterministic.
  • Once per frame: polling OS events, rendering, UI, audio mixing, camera smoothing that's purely visual.
  • Input: sample once per frame. Make sure a "just pressed" event is consumed by exactly one step (the jump buffer in Coin Dash does this neatly).
