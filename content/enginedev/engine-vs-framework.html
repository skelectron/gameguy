The "who calls whom" test

LibraryFrameworkEngine
Who owns main() and the loop?YouYou (or a thin base class)The engine
How do you add behaviour?Call functionsCompose packages, sometimes override hooksAttach scripts to engine objects
Editor?NoUsually noYes: scenes, assets, inspector
ExamplesGLFW, SDL, Box2D, stb_imageBRUTAL, MonoGame/FNA, raylib, LÖVEUnity, Godot, Unreal, GameMaker

BRUTAL is a framework built out of libraries. Each package (Brutal.Box2D, Brutal.Glfw…) is close to a plain library, and BRUTAL as a whole is the curated, consistent set. Its maintainers say they won't add an editor, a scene graph or a renderer, because that layer is what you, or a studio like Ludeon with Waylith, build on top.

Why build your own engine layer?

  • The game is unusual: huge simulations (RimWorld, Factorio), voxel worlds, orbital physics (Kitten Space Agency).
  • Longevity: a game supported for 10+ years can't depend on another company's roadmap.
  • Performance: data layouts designed for your game.
  • Learning: there's no better way to understand engines than to build one.
THE TRAP

"I'll build an engine and then make my game" usually ends with an engine and no game. The healthy approach is to build the game, then pull the engine out of it. Write only what this game needs, and only generalise code once you've needed it twice.
