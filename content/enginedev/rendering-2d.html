2D rendering: sprites, batches & cameras

BRUTAL deliberately has no renderer, which means writing one is your job, and it's one of the most rewarding parts of engine work. A 2D renderer has a simple goal: draw thousands of textured rectangles with as few draw calls as possible.

Why batch?

Every OpenGl.Draw call has a CPU cost (state validation and driver work). 5,000 sprites drawn one at a time means 5,000 draw calls, which can be slow. The same 5,000 sprites in one vertex buffer is 1 draw call. DemoBox2D.cs already does this: it writes every box into one vertices array and calls Draw once.

A sprite batch

A batch breaks, forcing a Flush, whenever the texture or shader changes. That's why games pack sprites into a texture atlas, one big image holding many sprites, so that a whole level can draw from one texture.

Draw order

  • Layers: background → tiles → entities → particles → UI. Draw them in order (the painter's algorithm).
  • Y-sorting for top-down games: sort sprites by their feet's Y position so characters walk behind trees properly.
  • Sort by (layer, texture) to keep batches large.

Cameras

1 - Exp(-k·dt) gives you smoothing that behaves the same at any frame rate. A plain Lerp(a, b, 0.1f) is faster at 144 FPS than at 60.

Pixel-perfect rendering

  1. Render the world into a small offscreen framebuffer (e.g. 320×180) with GL_NEAREST textures.
  2. Draw that framebuffer to the window at the largest integer scale that fits, and letterbox the rest. This is the same idea as SDL's IntegerScale (see the SDL lesson).
  3. Round the camera and sprite positions to whole world pixels.
