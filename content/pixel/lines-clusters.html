Lines, clusters & readability

Clean lines follow a pattern

A pixel line looks smooth when its segments follow a consistent pattern (1-1-1, 2-2-2, 1-2-1-2…). Breaking the pattern creates jaggies: visible bumps that pull the eye.

For curves, step lengths should change gradually: 3-2-1-1-2-3, not 3-1-3.

Avoid doubles and orphans

  • Doubles: L-shaped corners in a 1-pixel line make it look thick in places. Remove the inside corner pixel.
  • Orphan pixels: single stray pixels of a colour read as noise. Group colours into clusters, connected areas that read as one shape or material.

Readability first

  1. Silhouette: fill your sprite with solid black. Can you still tell what it is? If not, change the shape before you add detail.
  2. Outlines: a dark outline separates the sprite from busy backgrounds. A selective outline (lighter on the lit side) looks softer.
  3. Contrast: the player character should stand out more than anything else on screen.
  4. Exaggeration: heads, hands and weapons are often drawn bigger, so they read at 16 px.
EXERCISE

Draw a 16×16 sword at 45°, then at 30° (a 2:1 line). Check every line for jaggies and doubles. Then draw a 16×16 character silhouette that's recognisable when filled with a single colour.
