Scenes & state stacks

Real games have screens: the BRUTAL logo splash, a title screen, gameplay, a pause menu over gameplay, game over and credits. A scene stack handles all of them cleanly.

A typical flow

  • Pause is an overlay. Gameplay is still drawn underneath it but doesn't update, so the game freezes behind a dimmed menu for free.
  • Operations are queued, so a scene can safely Pop() itself during its own Update.
  • Transitions such as fades and wipes can be overlay scenes too.

Scene = data + systems

In bigger engines, a "scene" also owns a world (entities, physics world, audio sources). Loading a level means creating a new world, and leaving means disposing it. Because Box2D.CreateWorld is IDisposable and disposing it destroys all its bodies, a gameplay scene can hold the physics world and dispose it in Exit().
